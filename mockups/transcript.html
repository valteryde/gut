A — Timeline proposal

A mint spine down the left edge turns the transcript into one continuous timeline: filled dots for messages, squares for cards, clay for questions, red for errors, a pulsing node on the live row. Structured entries become raised white cards with an eyebrow header inside — the label column is gone, so text gets the full width. Most structure of the four; the rail also gives verbose mode a natural dotted-marker stream.

B — Bubbles proposal

The familiar chat shape: agent beats in paper-grey bubbles on the left, yours in clay on the right, a stamped sender line with the two brand dots over each beat. Plan and helper wear mint header strips with pill status; the question is a clay bubble with a "waiting" footnote; compaction shrinks to a centered chip; the live row is a real typing bubble. Friendliest, but the most conventional — and bubbles cost horizontal space.

C — Ledger proposal

The opposite move — unbox everything. Each beat is a ruled mono eyebrow ("GPT · plan ──────") over plain text; your messages are clay-tick quotes; the helper collapses to a named, ruled block; the only card left is the file delivery, because it's the one thing you click. The question keeps its full-bleed clay band. Quietest and most paper-native; risk is lower scannability when beats pile up.
